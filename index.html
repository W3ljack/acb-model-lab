The project

One league, every box score, tested models

ACB Model Lab is an independent analytics project on Spain's Liga Endesa, one of the strongest basketball leagues outside the NBA. It started as a question about player projections and grew into a full pipeline: every ACB box score back to the mid-1980s, shot charts for ten seasons, referee assignments matched across 25 years, attendance, closing betting odds from BetExplorer (used only to benchmark the model) and the 2026-27 calendar, all in one database.

Every model on this page was tested the same way: predict a season it has never seen, then compare. Ideas that did not survive that test were dropped, and the misses that remain are shown, not hidden. The page updates after every round.

    The 15-season replay

    Each past season predicted using only the seasons before it.

    Close to the betting market, not ahead of it: on 4,288 games with closing odds, the market's favourite won 72.1% of the time and the in-season model's pick 70.9%.

    Where the betting market numbers come from

    Source: BetExplorer (betexplorer.com), which publishes the average closing odds across bookmakers for every ACB game. "Closing" means the last price before tip-off, after the injury news and late money are in. No single bookmaker is used.

    Coverage: regular-season games from 2011-12 to 2025-26. Each game was matched to our database by season and clubs and kept only if BetExplorer's final score agreed exactly with the official ACB box score: 4,288 games.

    Turning odds into a probability: decimal odds of 1.50 imply a 1 / 1.50 = 67% chance. The two sides of a game add up to about 105% because of the bookmakers' margin, so both are scaled down to add to 100%. That is the market's win probability, and the market's pick is whichever side it favours.

    The comparison: on exactly the same games, the model's in-season prediction (made using only games before that date) and the market's closing probability are scored against the result, by share of winners picked and by log loss.

    Referees

    Investigation

    The home whistle is real, and it vanished when the fans did

    Over 25 seasons, away teams are called for more fouls and home teams shoot more free throws. Crowd size makes no difference, but during the pandemic, when the 2020-21 season was played without fans, the edge disappeared. A handful of referees also call noticeably more or fewer fouls than their peers.

    The home whistle, season by season

    Away team's fouls minus the home team's, per game, with 95% intervals. Above zero favours the home side.

    By the numbers

    Referee by referee

    How the referee effects are measured

    Each game's fouls per 100 possessions is modelled with the season, playoffs and overtime, both clubs' level that season (home and away separately), and an indicator for each of the three referees. Referee effects are shrunk toward the average referee (ridge regression, penalty chosen by 5-fold cross-validation), with 90% bootstrap intervals.

    Clubs and season explain R² 0.36 of the game-to-game variation in fouls; adding the referees takes it to 0.385. Small, but real: F(83, 6906) = 3.36, p < 0.001, and 500 shuffles of the crews never produced differences this large (permutation p = 0.002). Colored referees differ from the average after correcting for testing all of them (Benjamini-Hochberg, q < 0.10).

    An effect is relative to the average referee, so it says nothing about fairness. A lean every referee shares is invisible here; the season chart above measures that instead.

    By the numbers

    Shooting

    Where every player takes his shots
    Shot profiles

    Shooters travel. Inside scoring doesn't.

    A player's share of threes barely moves when he changes club. How he gets his twos, at the rim or in the paint and mid-range, depends on the new team's system. That is the part only film can fill in.

    How well last season predicts this one

    For every player with 150+ shots in 2023-24, 2024-25 or 2025-26, we used his shot mix from the season before to predict it. We scored those predictions with R² (R-squared): the share of the differences between players' shot mixes that the prediction explains. 100% would be perfect, but about 95% is the realistic ceiling, because where a player's shots come from always has some luck in it.
